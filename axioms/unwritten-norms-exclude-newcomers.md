# Unwritten norms exclude newcomers

## Claim

Implicit rules are legible only to existing members. Making norms explicit is an act of inclusion, not bureaucracy.

## Justification

Every community has norms that its established members learned by osmosis — what topics are welcomed, what tone registers as rude, how disagreement is handled, when humor is appropriate, which kinds of questions will be answered generously and which will be mocked. For existing members, these norms feel natural and not worth writing down. For newcomers, they are invisible until violated, at which point the newcomer is corrected, ignored, or shunned for rules they had no way of knowing.

Freeman's (1972) *The Tyranny of Structurelessness* made this case about unstructured political groups and it has aged well. The absence of formal structure does not produce equality; it produces informal structure, visible to insiders and invisible to outsiders, in which power accrues to those who know how the group actually works. The same pattern appears in online communities. The unwritten-norm regime is often defended as preserving community culture, avoiding bureaucratic overhead, or keeping things organic. In practice it systematically advantages people who already match the existing membership and disadvantages everyone else. People who share the prior context of the established members — demographic, cultural, professional — learn the norms quickly because the norms are familiar. People who don't, don't. The community reproduces its existing composition, not by intent but by the opacity of its rules.

Making norms explicit is not only fair — it is effective. Matias (2019), in a pre-registered field experiment across 2,190 Reddit discussions, found that posting rules at the start of a discussion increased newcomer participation by 38% and reduced rule-violating behavior, without any enforcement action. Making expectations visible shifts behavior on its own. The work of writing norms down falls on people who already understand them and therefore do not personally feel the need. This asymmetry is why the work is usually not done. It is also why not doing it is a choice with consequences, even when no individual is choosing.

## Implications

- Explicit norms are an inclusion mechanism. Implicit norms are an exclusion mechanism. The choice to leave norms implicit is a choice about who can comfortably join.
- The primary audience for written rules and guidelines is newcomers and peripheral members, not the established core who already know the norms.
- Writing norms down often exposes disagreements the established core has quietly papered over. This is a feature of the process, not a cost — the disagreement was already there, just not legible.
- Norms that cannot survive being written down plainly probably should not be enforced. If a norm is defensible only when implicit, that is a signal it would not survive scrutiny.
