# Axioms

An axiom here is a stated commitment — a belief about how online communities work that the rest of this project's work is built on. Axioms are not empirical findings. They may be informed by evidence, but they are the priors that shape what evidence we find relevant and how we interpret it.

When we take a position elsewhere in this repo that depends on one of these commitments, we cite the axiom. When evidence challenges an axiom, we revise the axiom rather than quietly contradicting ourselves. The point is to keep our foundations legible, contestable, and revisable.

These are starting points. We expect to add, revise, and occasionally retire axioms as the work continues.

## Index

- [Chat is witnessed asynchronously](./chat-is-witnessed-asynchronously.md) — messages are read by more people over more time than the sender anticipates.
- [Moderation is seen by everyone](./moderation-is-seen-by-everyone.md) — every enforcement action is a public message about what this community actually is.
- [Affiliation is mostly peripheral](./affiliation-is-mostly-peripheral.md) — most members lurk, occasionally participate, or self-identify; they are not contributors.
- [Scale changes the mechanics of trust](./scale-changes-trust.md) — small-group trust models do not scale linearly; large communities require legible process.
- [Unwritten norms exclude newcomers](./unwritten-norms-exclude-newcomers.md) — making norms explicit is an act of inclusion, not bureaucracy.
- [The platform is not neutral](./the-platform-is-not-neutral.md) — platform affordances shape what governance and engagement can exist.

## Format

Each axiom file contains:

1. **Claim** — a one-sentence statement of the commitment.
2. **Justification** — 2-4 paragraphs explaining why we hold this belief.
3. **Implications** — what following this axiom means for our work.

Axioms are short by design. If an axiom needs more than ~400 words to defend, it probably contains more than one claim and should be split.
