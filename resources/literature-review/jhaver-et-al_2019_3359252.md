# Does Transparency in Moderation Really Matter?: User Behavior After Content Removal Explanations on Reddit

## Abstract

When posts are removed on a social media platform, users may or may not receive an explanation. What effects does receiving an explanation have on the user? Using a dataset of 32 million Reddit posts, we characterize the removal explanations that are provided to Redditors, and link these explanations to measures of subsequent user behaviors—including future post submissions and future post removals. We show that compared to offering no explanation, offering an explanation for removal is associated with a lower rate of post-rejection in the future. We also show that removal explanations that are considered "fair" by the community (and thus are not downvoted) are linked to even lower future post-rejection rates, while removal explanations that are considered "unfair" (downvoted) do not significantly differ from not providing an explanation at all. We further find evidence suggesting that explanation length and the use of "we" in explanations are linked to lower future post-rejection rates, but the evidence is not conclusive. Our work suggests that transparency in the form of providing explanations for content moderation actions can improve user behavior. This has direct implications for the design of content moderation systems.

## AI Summary

Jhaver, Bruckman, and Gilbert (2019) conducted a large-scale observational study examining the effects of providing removal explanations to users on Reddit whose posts had been taken down by moderators. The study analyzed a dataset of 32 million Reddit posts, making it one of the most comprehensive empirical investigations of transparency as an enforcement mechanism in online content moderation.

The research methodology involved characterizing the removal explanations that moderators provided to Reddit users and then linking those explanations to measures of subsequent user behavior. The key behavioral outcomes measured were future post submissions (whether users continued participating) and future post removals (whether users' subsequent posts were also removed, serving as a proxy for improved rule compliance). By comparing users who received explanations with those who did not, the researchers could assess whether transparency in enforcement led to measurable behavior change.

The central finding is that offering an explanation for why a post was removed is associated with a lower rate of post-rejection in the future. This suggests that when users understand why their content was removed, they adjust their behavior to better comply with community rules in subsequent posts. This alone is a significant finding, as it provides empirical support for the intuition that transparency improves compliance.

The study went further by examining the perceived fairness of explanations. Removal explanations that the community considered "fair" — operationalized as explanations that were not downvoted by other users — were linked to even lower future post-rejection rates. In contrast, removal explanations perceived as "unfair" — those that were downvoted by the community — showed no significant difference from providing no explanation at all. This finding has profound implications: it demonstrates that the quality and perceived legitimacy of enforcement communication matters as much as the act of communicating itself. Simply providing any explanation is not enough; the explanation must be perceived as fair and reasonable to be effective.

The researchers also explored the characteristics of effective explanations. They found suggestive evidence that explanation length and the use of inclusive language such as "we" (as opposed to more authoritarian or distancing language) were linked to lower future post-rejection rates, though these results were not conclusive and warrant further investigation.

This work is foundational for understanding transparency as an enforcement mechanism in online communities. It establishes that how enforcement actions are communicated matters as much as what is enforced. The practical implications are direct: community moderation systems should prioritize providing clear, fair, and well-crafted explanations when removing content. Systems that remove content silently or with perfunctory explanations miss an opportunity to improve user behavior. The study also highlights the importance of community perception in enforcement legitimacy — explanations that the community views as unfair may actually undermine the goals of moderation rather than advance them.
