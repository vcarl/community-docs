# The Great Ban: Analyzing User Behavioral Changes after Reddit's Subreddit Bans of 2020

## Abstract

In the current landscape of online abuses and harms, effective content moderation is necessary to cultivate safe and inclusive online spaces. On June 29, 2020, Reddit banned approximately 2,000 subreddits for violating its content policies, in what is colloquially known as "The Great Ban." In this work, we study the effects of The Great Ban on the behavior of 34,000 Reddit users who were active in the banned subreddits. Specifically, we analyze changes in users' activity levels, the toxicity of their comments, and their migration patterns to alternative platforms (such as Scored and Gab) or to similar subreddits. Our results suggest that The Great Ban was effective in reducing overall activity on Reddit. We also observe a small but significant decrease in toxicity among users who remained on the platform. However, we find that a non-trivial portion of these users migrated to fringe platforms where even fewer content moderation safeguards exist. Overall, our findings paint a complex picture of the effects of radical moderation interventions, with important implications for the design and implementation of content moderation strategies.

## AI Summary

Cima, Trujillo, Avvenuti, and Cresci (2024) studied "The Great Ban" -- Reddit's June 2020 mass ban of approximately 2,000 subreddits including r/The_Donald, r/ChapoTrapHouse, and others. This event represented one of the largest coordinated content moderation actions in platform history, providing a unique opportunity to study the effects of radical moderation interventions at scale. The researchers tracked 34,000 users who were active in the banned communities, analyzing their behavior across three key dimensions.

First, the study examined activity levels on Reddit following the bans. The findings show that overall Reddit activity among the tracked users decreased substantially after the ban event. Users who had been active in the banned subreddits posted and commented less frequently on the platform, suggesting that the bans successfully disrupted the engagement patterns of users who participated in communities that violated Reddit's content policies.

Second, the researchers analyzed the toxicity of comments made by users who remained on the platform after the bans. They observed a small but statistically significant decrease in toxicity among these users. This finding suggests that the bans had a moderating effect not only on where users participated but also on how they communicated -- users who stayed on Reddit adopted somewhat less toxic language in their subsequent interactions.

Third, and perhaps most critically, the study investigated migration patterns to alternative platforms. The researchers found that a non-trivial portion of users from banned communities migrated to fringe platforms such as Scored and Gab, where content moderation safeguards are significantly weaker or nearly nonexistent. This migration pattern raises important questions about whether platform-level bans effectively reduce harmful behavior in the broader online ecosystem or simply displace it to less regulated spaces.

This work builds on and extends earlier research in the field, particularly Chandrasekharan et al. (2017), who studied Reddit's earlier bans of r/fatpeoplehate and r/CoonTown, and Ribeiro et al. (2021), who documented migration patterns from banned communities to alternative platforms. By studying a much larger-scale ban event encompassing approximately 2,000 subreddits simultaneously, Cima et al. provide evidence about the effects of mass moderation interventions that go beyond the targeted removal of individual communities.

The findings reinforce an emerging pattern in content moderation research: mass bans are effective at reducing harmful content on the banning platform and can even slightly reform the behavior of some users who remain, but they also push a subset of users toward less moderated spaces where harmful behavior may intensify unchecked. This dynamic is important for understanding that content moderation operates as an ecosystem -- actions taken by one platform have ripple effects across the web. Platform governance decisions do not exist in isolation; they reshape the distribution of users and content across the broader online landscape.

The implications for content moderation strategy are significant. Platforms considering large-scale bans should anticipate migration to alternative spaces and consider how their actions interact with the broader ecosystem of online platforms. The study suggests that while bans are an effective tool for improving conditions on the banning platform, a comprehensive approach to reducing online harms requires coordination and awareness of cross-platform dynamics.
