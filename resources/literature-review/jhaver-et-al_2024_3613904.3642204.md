# Bystanders of Online Moderation: Examining the Effects of Witnessing Post-Removal Explanations

## Abstract

Prior research on transparency in content moderation has demonstrated the benefits of offering post-removal explanations to sanctioned users. However, less is known about how witnessing moderation actions influence bystanders' behaviors. We conducted two experimental studies (N1 = 361, N2 = 1,361) examining the effects of witnessing explanations of post removals on bystanders' decisions to post as well as their anticipated posting behaviors. Our first experiment compared the effects of witnessing post removals with or without explanations to a no-removal control condition. We found that post removal without explanation decreased bystanders' intention to post comments on the platform and that adding an explanation to removal did not change bystander behavioral intentions. Our second experiment investigated the effects of different explanation designs by varying the removal explanation's length and politeness. Here, we found that exposure to longer and polite removal explanations was associated with a greater willingness among bystanders to post. Overall, our results highlight that post removals can have chilling effects on bystanders, and underscore the importance of providing well-crafted explanations to reduce these negative effects.

## AI Summary

Jhaver, Rathi, and Saha (2024) extended earlier research on transparency in content moderation by shifting the focus from the sanctioned user to bystanders — community members who witness moderation actions but are not themselves the target of enforcement. This is an important reframing because most community members experience moderation primarily as observers rather than as subjects, yet prior research had focused almost exclusively on the effects of moderation on the users whose content was removed.

The researchers conducted two experimental studies with substantial sample sizes. The first experiment (N = 361) compared three conditions: witnessing a post removal with an explanation, witnessing a post removal without an explanation, and a no-removal control condition. The second experiment (N = 1,361) investigated the effects of different explanation designs by varying two key dimensions of the removal explanation: its length and its politeness.

The findings from the first experiment revealed an important and concerning dynamic. Witnessing a post removal without an accompanying explanation decreased bystanders' intention to post comments on the platform — a "chilling effect" where enforcement actions suppressed participation among people who were not even the targets of moderation. Adding an explanation to the removal did not significantly change bystander behavioral intentions in this first experiment, meaning that simply providing any explanation was not sufficient to counteract the chilling effect.

The second experiment provided more nuanced and actionable results. When the researchers varied the design of the removal explanation, they found that exposure to longer and polite removal explanations was associated with a greater willingness among bystanders to post. This suggests that while the mere presence of an explanation may not be enough, the craft and tone of the explanation matter significantly. Longer explanations provide more context and reasoning, while polite language signals that the moderation team acts with care and respect rather than through arbitrary authority.

This paper is important for online community governance because it demonstrates that enforcement actions have ripple effects far beyond the individual violator. Heavy-handed or unexplained enforcement can suppress participation even among rule-following community members who simply observe moderation taking place. This creates a tension for community managers: enforcement is necessary to maintain community standards, but if done poorly, it can undermine the very participation and engagement that the community depends on.

The practical implications are clear. Communities should invest in crafting removal explanations that are longer, more detailed, and polite in tone. Terse or unexplained removals risk creating an atmosphere of unpredictability and fear that discourages bystanders from contributing. The findings reinforce that moderation is not just a behind-the-scenes enforcement activity but a form of public communication that shapes the overall community climate and willingness of members to participate.
