# Literature Review Reading List (v1)

Candidate papers for the governance & member engagement literature review, organized by axiom. Citations verified 2026-04-14.

35 unique papers. Tyler (2006) anchors both A2 and A4; all other entries appear once.

This is a checkpoint, not a final reading list. Entries may be added, removed, or have their framing revised as summaries are written and axioms evolve.

---

## A1 — Chat is witnessed asynchronously

See [`axioms/chat-is-witnessed-asynchronously.md`](../axioms/chat-is-witnessed-asynchronously.md). Already cites Bernstein et al. 2013 and Marwick & boyd 2011.

1. **Herring, S. C. (1999).** Interactional Coherence in CMC. *Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication*, 4(4). https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1083-6101.1999.tb00106.x
   — Foundational analysis of how chat violates sequential turn-taking; persistence of the transcript forces meaning-reconstruction from logs rather than live exchange.

2. **boyd, d. (2010/2011).** Social Network Sites as Networked Publics: Affordances, Dynamics, and Implications. In Z. Papacharissi (Ed.), *A Networked Self: Identity, Community, and Culture on Social Network Sites* (pp. 39–58). Routledge.
   — Canonical affordance vocabulary (persistence, replicability, scalability, searchability) that names why online talk functions as broadcast even when it feels interpersonal.

3. **Litt, E., & Hargittai, E. (2016).** The Imagined Audience on Social Network Sites. *Social Media + Society*, 2(1). https://doi.org/10.1177/2056305116633482
   — Post-2015 survey evidence that the imagined audience is a small, specific subset of actual reach; direct empirical successor to Marwick & boyd.

4. **Bayer, J. B., Ellison, N. B., Schoenebeck, S. Y., & Falk, E. B. (2016).** Sharing the Small Moments: Ephemeral Social Interaction on Snapchat. *Information, Communication & Society*, 19(7), 956–977. https://doi.org/10.1080/1369118X.2015.1084349
   — Ephemerality as license for less audience-managed talk; sharpens the axiom by naming what chat's permanence normatively does. Boundary condition.

## A2 — Moderation is seen by everyone

See [`axioms/moderation-is-seen-by-everyone.md`](../axioms/moderation-is-seen-by-everyone.md). Already cites Jhaver 2019/2024, Seering 2019, Frluckaj & Howison 2024 (all in `literature-review/`).

1. **Tyler, T. R. (2006).** *Why People Obey the Law* (2nd ed.). Princeton University Press.
   — Canonical procedural-justice anchor: perceived fairness of process drives compliance and legitimacy more than outcome favorability or deterrent severity. (Shared with A4.)

2. **Tyler, T. R., & Huo, Y. J. (2002).** *Trust in the Law: Encouraging Public Cooperation with the Police and Courts*. Russell Sage Foundation.
   — Extends procedural justice to third-party observers: community members form legitimacy judgments from witnessed enforcement without personal contact.

3. **Nagin, D. S. (2013).** Deterrence in the Twenty-First Century: A Review of the Evidence. *Crime and Justice*, 42(1), 199–263. https://doi.org/10.1086/670398
   — Modern deterrence synthesis: certainty and perceived legitimacy outperform severity; deterrent effects operate largely through observers updating beliefs about enforcement.

4. **Myers West, S. (2018).** Censored, Suspended, Shadowbanned: User Interpretations of Content Moderation on Social Media Platforms. *New Media & Society*, 20(11), 4366–4383. https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444818773059
   — Interview study documenting folk theories and chilling effects that form when moderation is invisible or unexplained. Broadens chilling-effect evidence beyond Jhaver.

5. **Suzor, N. P., West, S. M., Quodling, A., & York, J. (2019).** What Do We Mean When We Talk About Transparency? Toward Meaningful Transparency in Commercial Content Moderation. *International Journal of Communication*, 13, 1526–1543.
   — Boundary condition: transparency must be evaluated by users' ability to understand and contest decisions, not by disclosure volume. Visibility without intelligibility does not produce legitimacy.

6. **Foucault, M. (1977).** *Discipline and Punish: The Birth of the Prison* (A. Sheridan, Trans.). Pantheon Books.
   — Canonical treatment of punishment-as-spectacle and the disciplinary shift toward visibility; enforcement as communicative act directed at the watching population. Humanities counterweight to the HCI empirical literature.

7. **Pan, C. A., Yakhmi, S., Iyer, T. P., Strasnick, E., Zhang, A. X., & Bernstein, M. S. (2022).** Comparing the Perceived Legitimacy of Content Moderation Processes: Contractors, Algorithms, Expert Panels, and Digital Juries. *Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction*, 6(CSCW1), Article 82. https://doi.org/10.1145/3512929
   — Experimentally varies moderation procedure and measures third-party legitimacy judgments; an empirical test of procedural-justice predictions in a platform context.

## A3 — Affiliation is mostly peripheral

See [`axioms/affiliation-is-mostly-peripheral.md`](../axioms/affiliation-is-mostly-peripheral.md). Already cites Lave & Wenger 1991 and Nonnecke & Preece 2000.

1. **Wenger, E. (1998).** *Communities of Practice: Learning, Meaning, and Identity*. Cambridge University Press.
   — Wenger's solo follow-up to Lave & Wenger 1991; develops "modes of belonging" (engagement, imagination, alignment) giving peripheral/imagined affiliation theoretical standing distinct from contribution.

2. **Ren, Y., Kraut, R., & Kiesler, S. (2007).** Applying Common Identity and Bond Theory to Design of Online Communities. *Organization Studies*, 28(3), 377–408. https://doi.org/10.1177/0170840607076007
   — Distinguishes identity-based from bond-based attachment. Peripheral affiliation at scale is predominantly identity-based; provides mechanism for why it is stable rather than transitional.

3. **Sun, N., Rau, P.-L. P., & Ma, L. (2014).** Understanding Lurkers in Online Communities: A Literature Review. *Computers in Human Behavior*, 38, 110–117. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2014.05.022
   — Synthesizes ~a decade of post-Nonnecke lurker research; reframes lurking from deficit model toward legitimate participatory mode.

4. **Antin, J., & Cheshire, C. (2010).** Readers Are Not Free-Riders: Reading as a Form of Participation on Wikipedia. *Proceedings of CSCW 2010*, 127–130. https://doi.org/10.1145/1718918.1718942
   — Empirical argument that reading is participation with value to readers' identity formation and to the community. Concrete support for non-contribution-centric governance.

5. **Kraut, R. E., & Resnick, P. (2012).** *Building Successful Online Communities: Evidence-Based Social Design*. MIT Press. https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/8472.001.0001
   — Canonical HCI synthesis; chapters on commitment systematically treat identity-based vs. bond-based attachment and design implications for non-posting members.

6. **Seering, J., Ng, F., Yao, Z., & Kaufman, G. (2018).** Applications of Social Identity Theory to Research and Design in Computer-Supported Cooperative Work. *Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction*, 2(CSCW), Article 201. https://doi.org/10.1145/3274771
   — Applies social identity theory to CSCW contexts, including claimed affiliation without active participation — the dynamic the Mozilla "claiming affiliation" clause responds to.

## A4 — Scale changes the mechanics of trust

See [`axioms/scale-changes-trust.md`](../axioms/scale-changes-trust.md). Already cites Dunbar 1992, Ostrom 1990, and Kraut & Resnick 2012.

1. **Tyler, T. R. (2006).** *Why People Obey the Law* (2nd ed.). Princeton University Press.
   — (Shared with A2.) Canonical theoretical anchor for the "legible process" mechanism: members infer trustworthiness from consistency and transparent explanation.

2. **Resnick, P., Kuwabara, K., Zeckhauser, R., & Friedman, E. (2000).** Reputation Systems. *Communications of the ACM*, 43(12), 45–48. https://doi.org/10.1145/355112.355122
   — Canonical argument that engineered reputation systems substitute for interpersonal familiarity as the trust substrate in large online populations.

3. **O'Mahony, S., & Ferraro, F. (2007).** The Emergence of Governance in an Open Source Community. *Academy of Management Journal*, 50(5), 1079–1106. https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2007.27169153
   — Longitudinal Debian study of phased retooling from informal to bureaucratic + democratic mechanisms as the community scaled. Documents the mechanism.

4. **Forte, A., Larco, V., & Bruckman, A. (2009).** Decentralization in Wikipedia Governance. *Journal of Management Information Systems*, 26(1), 49–72. https://doi.org/10.2753/MIS0742-1222260103
   — Wikipedia governance decentralization framed through Ostrom; empirical bridge from commons theory to online community. Demonstrates the "legible process" substrate.

5. **Shaw, A., & Hill, B. M. (2014).** Laboratories of Oligarchy? How the Iron Law Extends to Peer Production. *Journal of Communication*, 64(2), 215–238. https://doi.org/10.1111/jcom.12082
   — Analysis of 683 wikis showing growth concentrates authority among early members. Boundary condition: process alone may not neutralize oligarchic drift.

6. **Small, C. T., Bjorkegren, M., Erkkilä, T., Shaw, L., & Megill, C. (2021).** Polis: Scaling Deliberation by Mapping High Dimensional Opinion Spaces. *Recerca: Revista de Pensament i Anàlisi*, 26(2). https://doi.org/10.6035/recerca.5516
   — Post-2020 technical substrate for process-based legitimacy at scale (vTaiwan and related deployments). Operationalizes deliberation at the axiom's scale.

## A5 — Unwritten norms exclude newcomers

See [`axioms/unwritten-norms-exclude-newcomers.md`](../axioms/unwritten-norms-exclude-newcomers.md). Already cites Freeman 1972 and Matias 2019.

1. **Joyce, E., & Kraut, R. E. (2006).** Predicting Continued Participation in Newsgroups. *Journal of Computer-Mediated Communication*, 11(3), 723–747. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1083-6101.2006.00033.x
   — Classic newcomer-socialization study: the nature of early responses strongly predicts whether newcomers return. Mechanism for how implicit norms filter who stays.

2. **Halfaker, A., Geiger, R. S., Morgan, J. T., & Riedl, J. (2013).** The Rise and Decline of an Open Collaborative System: How Wikipedia's Reaction to Popularity Is Causing Its Decline. *American Behavioral Scientist*, 57(5), 664–688. https://doi.org/10.1177/0002764212469365
   — Canonical empirical case: as Wikipedia's informal norms calcified into semi-automated gatekeeping, newcomer retention collapsed.

3. **Morgan, J. T., Bouterse, S., Walls, H., & Stierch, S. (2013).** Tea and Sympathy: Crafting Positive New User Experiences on Wikipedia. *Proceedings of CSCW 2013*, 839–848. https://doi.org/10.1145/2441776.2441871
   — Teahouse intervention: converting tacit welcoming norms into explicit scaffolding measurably improved retention and participation.

4. **Bryant, S. L., Forte, A., & Bruckman, A. (2005).** Becoming Wikipedian: Transformation of Participation in a Collaborative Online Encyclopedia. *Proceedings of GROUP 2005*, 1–10. https://doi.org/10.1145/1099203.1099205
   — LPP applied to an online community. Competing framing: some tacitness is load-bearing and part of what makes deep membership meaningful.

5. **Kiene, C., Monroy-Hernández, A., & Hill, B. M. (2016).** Surviving an "Eternal September": How an Online Community Managed a Surge of Newcomers. *Proceedings of CHI 2016*, 1152–1156. https://doi.org/10.1145/2858036.2858356
   — r/NoSleep case study on making previously tacit expectations explicit under newcomer-influx pressure. Explicit norms help, but paired with visible enforcement infrastructure.

6. **Seering, J., Dym, B., Kaufman, G., & Bernstein, M. (2022).** Pride and Professionalization in Volunteer Moderation: Lessons for Effective Platform-User Collaboration. *Journal of Online Trust and Safety*, 1(2). https://tsjournal.org/index.php/jots/article/view/34
   — Post-2020 examination of how volunteer moderator cultures develop internal norms and jargon opaque to the communities they serve. Informal structure as barrier to equitable participation.

## A6 — The platform is not neutral

See [`axioms/the-platform-is-not-neutral.md`](../axioms/the-platform-is-not-neutral.md). Already cites Winner 1980, Lessig 2006, and Gillespie 2010.

1. **Grimmelmann, J. (2015).** The Virtues of Moderation. *Yale Journal of Law & Technology*, 17, 42–109.
   — Legal-scholarship taxonomy of moderation techniques (exclusion, pricing, organizing, norm-setting); argues platform architecture determines which are feasible.

2. **Gillespie, T. (2018).** *Custodians of the Internet: Platforms, Content Moderation, and the Hidden Decisions That Shape Social Media*. Yale University Press.
   — Book-length development of Gillespie 2010. Moderation is constitutive of platforms, not incidental; platform design and policy invisibly govern public discourse.

3. **Suzor, N. P. (2019).** *Lawless: The Secret Rules That Govern Our Digital Lives*. Cambridge University Press. https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108666428
   — Frames platform governance through rule-of-law deficits (opacity, inconsistency, lack of due process); contractual/technical architectures foreclose accountability communities might otherwise build.

4. **Nagy, P., & Neff, G. (2015).** Imagined Affordances: Reconstructing a Keyword for Communication Theory. *Social Media + Society*, 1(2). https://doi.org/10.1177/2056305115603385
   — Important refinement: affordances emerge from designer intent + materiality + user imagination. Guards the axiom against hard technological determinism.

5. **Plantin, J.-C., Lagoze, C., Edwards, P. N., & Sandvig, C. (2018).** Infrastructure Studies Meet Platform Studies in the Age of Google and Facebook. *New Media & Society*, 20(1), 293–310. https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444816661553
   — Platforms exhibit infrastructural qualities (invisibility-until-breakdown, embedded standards) while remaining privately controlled. Underwrites the "platform feels like a natural environment" observation.

6. **Kiene, C., Jiang, J. A., & Hill, B. M. (2019).** Technological Frames and User Innovation: Exploring Technological Change in Community Moderation Teams. *Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction*, 3(CSCW), Article 44. https://doi.org/10.1145/3359146
   — Empirical CSCW study of Reddit and Discord volunteer moderation teams building governance atop platform tooling; documents what the platform enabled and what admins had to build externally.

7. **Schneider, N. (2022).** Admins, Mods, and Benevolent Dictators for Life: The Implicit Feudalism of Online Communities. *New Media & Society*, 24(9), 1965–1985. https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444820986553
   — Names the governance-form constraint of platform defaults: platforms ship with owner/admin hierarchies that foreclose more democratic forms unless communities actively resist. Strong post-2020 anchor.
